01 / The trajectory

From a widening gap to a deadline.

The college’s latest projections show a smaller deficit each year. Reaching the target depends in part on enrollment goals being met.

FY2025 · CLOSED

$9.6 million

The reported deficit followed several years of enrollment pressure and expenses outpacing revenue.

FY2026 · PROJECTED

$8 million

Administrators say about 70% of expense reductions are happening in this fiscal year.

FY2027 · PROJECTED

$4 million

The college expects the remaining gap to shrink, with assumptions tied to incoming student numbers.

FY2028 · TARGET

Balanced

Administrators say the goal is to eliminate the deficit through expense reductions and revenue growth.

02 / The campus impact

Where the cuts meet campus life.

Financial changes show up in everyday decisions: how clubs plan events, who can travel, and how the college supports its programs and people.

“The college cannot cut its way to success.”President La Jerne Cornish, as reported by ABACC
01
Student organizations · Spring 2026

A $20,000 cut, with little notice

The SGC Appropriations Committee’s spring budget was reduced by $20,000. The Ithacan reported that more than 20 organizations were waitlisted, with groups scrambling to fund showcases and events.

Read The Ithacan’s report ↗
02
Travel funding · Beginning August 2026

Out-of-county trips lose support

OSE and SGC plan to end funding for student-organization travel outside Tompkins County. The anticipated reduction to the SGC appropriations budget is $55,000. Some clubs say trips build professional and community connections.

Read The Ithacan’s report ↗
03
Budget strategy · Ongoing

Expense cuts and new revenue

Reported measures include a faculty hiring freeze, higher parking-pass prices and reductions to student travel support. Administrators also point to enrollment and revenue growth as part of the plan.

Read The Ithacan’s report ↗
04
Enrollment · The key assumption

A smaller incoming class than the past

ABACC’s account of the earlier planning study says Fall 2024 brought 1,128 new students, around 200 below target. The college’s more recent plan relies on reaching its enrollment goals.

Read ABACC’s account ↗
03 / The underlying challenge

Enrollment shapes the math.

Tuition revenue is linked to the number of students who enroll. College leaders say their path to balance depends on hitting incoming class targets.
